Most recipe sites assume you will shop for the recipe. I usually want the opposite: given half a bag of spinach, some feta, and eggs, what can I make tonight?

The recommender works from a public set of about 60,000 recipes. Ingredient names are messy, so the first step normalizes them: "2 cups baby spinach, washed" becomes "spinach". A sentence-transformer model embeds each normalized ingredient, which lets the ranker treat "scallion" and "green onion" as the same thing and "kale" as a reasonable stand-in for "spinach".

Each recipe gets a score from the share of its ingredients the pantry covers, with a penalty for each missing item weighted by how central that item is to the dish. Salt and oil cost almost nothing to miss. The main protein costs a lot.

A small React front end lets me tick items off a pantry list and see the top twenty recipes update as I go. The ranking is good enough that I use it a couple of times a week. The pantry list is the weak point, because I forget to update it, and a scanner for barcodes or receipts would do more for the project than any model change.
