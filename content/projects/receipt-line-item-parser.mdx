I wanted my grocery spending broken down by category without typing every receipt into a spreadsheet. Existing apps either read only the total or sent the photo to a server I did not control.

The pipeline has two stages. Tesseract reads the image and returns words with their bounding boxes. A small LayoutLM-style token classifier then labels each word as part of a product name, a quantity, a unit price, a line total, or noise, using both the text and its position on the page. A few rules stitch the labeled words back into rows.

I labeled 340 receipts from five stores by hand, which took most of two weekends. On held-out receipts from those same stores, about nine in ten line items come out exactly right. On a store the model has never seen, that drops to roughly six in ten, mostly because abbreviations like "GRN BNS FRZ" vary so much between chains.

The weak point is the OCR, not the classifier. Faded thermal paper produces garbage that no amount of labeling can fix. The next step, if I return to it, is a better image cleanup pass before Tesseract ever sees the photo.
