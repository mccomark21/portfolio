## The problem

Every month the accounting team received around 1,100 supplier invoices. Each one had to be matched to a purchase order and to a record that the goods actually arrived, the classic three-way match. The work happened in spreadsheets exported from the ERP, and the team spent most of the first week of each month on it.

## What we built

A nightly job pulls open purchase orders, receipts, and newly scanned invoices from the ERP's reporting database. A matcher tries an exact match on PO number and amount first, then a tolerance match that allows small differences in freight and rounding, then a partial match for invoices that cover only some lines of an order.

Anything the matcher clears goes straight to the approved queue. Everything else lands in a review screen that says exactly why it failed: amount over tolerance, quantity received short, PO closed, or no PO found. The reviewer can accept, reject, or split an invoice across two orders with one form.

## What mattered

The reason codes mattered more than the match rate. Before, an unmatched invoice was just a red row. Now the team can see that most failures for one supplier are short receipts, and they call the warehouse instead of the supplier.

We kept the tolerance rules in a table the accounting lead can edit, rather than in code. She changed them twice in the first month, both times for good reasons we would not have guessed.
