The utility's app shows a bar chart of monthly use. By the time a bar looks tall, the money is already spent.

My meter exposes fifteen-minute readings through the utility's data download. A cron job on a Raspberry Pi pulls them every morning into DuckDB. The model is deliberately simple: an STL decomposition separates the daily and weekly cycles from the trend, and a reading is flagged when the residual stays above a rolling threshold for more than an hour. A single spike from the oven is not an anomaly. Two hours of steady extra draw at 3 a.m. probably is.

Eleven alerts so far. Seven had a real cause I could find, including a sump pump float switch that stuck after a storm and a space heater left on in the garage. Four were false alarms, mostly on days when we had guests.

The next step is weather. Cooling load follows outdoor temperature closely, and the current baseline treats a heat wave as an anomaly.
