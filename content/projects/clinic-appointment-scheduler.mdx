## The clinic

The clinic had three physical therapists, one front-desk coordinator, and a paper calendar that lived next to the phone. Every booking, change, and cancellation went through the coordinator. When she was out sick, the calendar effectively stopped.

## What I built

Patients get a link that shows open slots for their therapist over the next three weeks. They pick one, confirm with a code sent to their phone, and receive a reminder text the day before. Replying "C" to the reminder cancels the slot and returns it to the open pool, which the coordinator sees immediately.

The therapists asked for one thing above all: no double bookings, ever. Every slot is a row with a unique constraint on provider and start time, and booking happens in a single transaction that either claims the row or fails. A failed claim shows the patient the next three open slots instead of an error.

## The hard parts

Recurring appointments were harder than expected. Many patients come twice a week for six weeks, and a single holiday or vacation week breaks the pattern. I modeled a series as a parent record with individual child appointments, so that moving one visit never silently moved the rest.

Time zones did not matter for the patients, but they mattered for the reminder job, which ran on a server in a different zone and sent the first week of reminders an hour early.

## Outcome

After two months, the no-show rate fell from about 14 percent to about 6 percent. The coordinator now spends her mornings on insurance paperwork rather than on the phone.
